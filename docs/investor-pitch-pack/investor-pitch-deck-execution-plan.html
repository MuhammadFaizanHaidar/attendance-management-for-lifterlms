Investor-Style Pitch Deck Execution Plan

Create a complete investor pitch package for the AI Work Verification and Auto-Invoicing Platform, with separate execution tracks for the internal team and for Cursor/model workflows.

Use this document to guide planning, source collection, deck writing, challenge review, and packaging.

  • Demo flow notes
  • Cursor prompt pack

Team-Owned Truth

Cursor-Driven Acceleration

2. Investment Story to Aim For

The company should be framed as an AI-assisted infrastructure layer between project delivery, time, and billing, not merely as a feature for invoice automation.

  • Services businesses lose margin and trust because work, time, and billing are fragmented
  • Verification, auditability, and billing confidence matter more than ever
  • AI is useful when combined with auditable rules and system-of-record integrations
  • Invoice automation is the wedge; benchmarking and intelligence become the moat

4. Full Team Execution Plan

TEAM WORKFLOW

Step 1 - Lock the narrative

  • Define the exact category or wedge you are entering
  • Agree on the strongest pain point to lead with
  • Decide what makes the company venture-scale if it works

Step 2 - Choose the investor audience

  • Generalist pre-seed
  • AI application investors
  • Fintech/ops workflow investors
  • Vertical SaaS investors

Step 3 - Gather proof

  • Customer pain examples
  • Prototype screenshots
  • Design partner notes
  • Pricing assumptions
  • Roadmap and integration list
  • Founder bios

Step 4 - Build the proof hierarchy

Rank claims by evidence strength. Strongest proof includes paying users, pilots, LOIs, active beta commitments, and measurable prototypes.

Step 5 - Draft the deck in plain text

For each slide define the title, key point, 3 bullets maximum, required visual, and source of proof.

Step 6 - Pressure test the story

Simulate investor pushback around market size, trust, defensibility, and whether the product is a feature or a category-worthy company.

CURSOR / MODEL WORKFLOW

Step 1 - Feed a complete brief

Give Cursor a structured master brief that includes product summary, target customer, Phase 1 scope, traction signals, pricing assumptions, team background, and open questions.

Step 2 - Generate positioning options

  • Ask for multiple one-line positioning statements
  • Ask for several category framings
  • Compare which framing sounds most venture-backable

Step 3 - Generate deck structures

  • Create 10-slide, 12-slide, and 14-slide outlines
  • Choose the right one for the fundraising stage

Step 4 - Draft each slide in layers

  • headline options
  • three concise bullets
  • visual/chart suggestions
  • missing-proof flags
  • speaker notes

Step 5 - Run skeptical review passes

Ask Cursor to critique the deck like an investor and rewrite weak lines without inventing new facts.

Step 6 - Create support materials

  • Q&A prep
  • intro email
  • post-meeting note
  • one-page memo
  • demo script

5. Rules for Working With Cursor

Rule: the team owns the truth, and Cursor owns acceleration.
  • Provide facts first and assumptions second
  • Ask for options, not a single answer
  • Never allow made-up metrics, traction, or market claims
  • Use Cursor for sharpening, condensing, reviewing, and adapting to audiences
  • Keep the deck business-led, not feature-led

6. Division of Responsibilities

Team Cursor
Strategy, proof, numbers, customer truth, market assumptions, product scope, fundraising logic, and final sign-off. Rewrite for clarity, create framing options, compress slide copy, run challenge passes, generate Q&A prep, and adapt the story to audience variants.

  • proof-led, not adjective-led

  • clear on why this team can win
  • strong on business outcomes, not just features
