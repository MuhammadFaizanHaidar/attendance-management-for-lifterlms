# Investor-Style Pitch Deck Execution Plan

## Purpose

This document explains, in detail, how to create an investor-style pitch deck for the **AI Work Verification and Auto-Invoicing Platform**, with a complete execution flow for:

1. The internal team
2. Cursor / AI model workflows

It is designed so the founders, product team, and operators can work in parallel while using Cursor and other models as drafting, refinement, and packaging accelerators.

---

## 1. Desired Output

The goal is not just to make slides. The goal is to produce an investor-ready pitch package that includes:

1. A clean investor-style deck
2. A clear fundraising narrative
3. A metrics-backed story
4. A product demo storyline
5. A Q&A prep layer for likely investor objections
6. Reusable prompts and source material for Cursor/models

### Final deliverables
- Investor deck outline
- Investor deck slide copy
- Short pitch version
- Long pitch version
- Speaker notes
- Metrics worksheet
- Risk and objection notes
- Demo flow notes
- Cursor prompt pack

---

## 2. Recommended Pitch Goal

The pitch deck should convince investors that this company is building:

### Core investment story
An AI-assisted infrastructure layer for service delivery verification, effort benchmarking, and invoice automation.

### Why investors should care
- Large service economy
- Manual billing pain is real and expensive
- Work verification is becoming more important as teams become more distributed
- Finance, delivery, and operations tools are fragmented
- AI can improve explainability and workflow speed if combined with auditable business logic

### Positioning angle
This is not just "AI for invoices."

It should be positioned as:

**A system of record and intelligence layer between project delivery, time, and billing.**

---

## 3. Investor Deck Structure

Use a 10- to 14-slide structure.

## Slide 1 - Vision
- Company/product name
- One-line positioning statement
- Short tagline

## Slide 2 - Problem
- Hours are logged in one place, work happens in another, billing happens in a third
- Companies cannot confidently verify whether billed work was supported and fairly priced
- Finance and delivery teams spend too much time reconciling systems

## Slide 3 - Why Now
- AI can summarize and explain work evidence
- Software services teams face more scrutiny on margins
- Tool integrations are easier than before
- Billing confidence and auditability are increasingly important

## Slide 4 - Solution
- Cross-system work verification
- Hours-to-evidence matching
- Invoice validation
- Auto-invoicing from approved work

## Slide 5 - Product Walkthrough
- How a user connects tools
- How verification works
- How invoice drafts are produced
- Where AI adds value

## Slide 6 - Market
- TAM/SAM/SOM framing
- Start with agencies and services firms
- Expand to broader project-based organizations

## Slide 7 - Business Model
- SaaS subscription
- Seat/workspace/project pricing options
- Premium analytics and finance controls

## Slide 8 - Traction or Early Validation
- Customer conversations
- Beta interest
- Pilot pipeline
- Early prototypes
- Internal usage insights

## Slide 9 - Go-to-Market
- Design partners
- Direct founder-led sales
- Niche service firms first
- Finance + delivery buyer strategy

## Slide 10 - Competitive Advantage
- Cross-system mapping
- Verification + invoicing in one workflow
- Audit trail
- Benchmarking layer
- Feedback loop from usage data

## Slide 11 - Product Roadmap
- Phase 1: verification + invoice draft generation
- Phase 2: beta expansion with OCR/email/internal benchmarks
- Phase 3: intelligence and benchmarking
- Phase 4: market benchmarks and advanced automation

## Slide 12 - Team
- Founder credibility
- Domain understanding
- Why this team can execute

## Slide 13 - Financial/Operating Model
- Key assumptions
- Revenue path
- Pricing model
- Efficiency narrative

## Slide 14 - Ask
- Raise amount if applicable
- Use of funds
- Milestones enabled by the round

## 4. Execution Model

The best execution model is to split work into two tracks:

### Track A - Team-owned truth
This is where facts come from.

### Track B - Cursor/model-assisted packaging
This is where narrative, wording, polishing, variations, and packaging happen.

### Rule
The team owns the truth. Cursor owns acceleration.

That means:
- Team provides evidence, decisions, numbers, and priorities
- Cursor helps shape, rewrite, compare, condense, and present

---

## 5. Full Team Execution Plan

## Step 1 - Define the fundraising narrative

The team should hold a focused working session and answer:
- What category are we creating or entering?
- What is the strongest pain point we are solving first?
- Why is now the right time?
- Why will this product become large?
- Why is our team the right one?

### Output
A one-page narrative brief with:
- company vision
- first market
- key pain point
- product promise
- why now
- initial wedge

---

## Step 2 - Decide the investor audience

The deck changes depending on the investor type.

### Possible audiences
- Pre-seed generalist SaaS investors
- Fintech / back-office workflow investors
- AI application investors
- Vertical SaaS investors
- Strategic angels with services, ERP, or finance experience

### Output
A target investor profile with:
- stage
- thesis fit
- check size
- what they care about most

---

## Step 3 - Gather proof and source data

The team should collect the raw facts needed for the deck.

### Data to collect
- customer interview notes
- common workflow pain points
- examples of broken billing workflows
- current process screenshots
- prototype screenshots
- integration list
- product roadmap
- expected pricing
- design partner conversations
- market sizing assumptions
- founder bios

### Output
A structured source folder or spreadsheet containing all inputs.

---

## Step 4 - Build the proof hierarchy

Investors care about proof more than adjectives.

Create a ranked proof hierarchy:

### Strongest proof
- paying customers
- signed pilots
- LOIs
- measurable prototypes
- active beta commitments

### Medium proof
- expert interviews
- product mockups
- strong domain data
- clear ROI assumptions

### Weak proof
- generic trend claims
- vague "AI opportunity" language

### Output
A proof table with:
- claim
- supporting evidence
- confidence level
- owner

---

## Step 5 - Write the core messaging pillars

The team should agree on 3 to 5 messaging pillars that every version of the deck must reinforce.

### Suggested pillars
1. Verification of work is becoming financially important
2. Delivery, time, and billing data are fragmented
3. Combining rules, integrations, and AI creates a defensible workflow
4. Invoice automation is the first wedge; benchmarking becomes the moat

### Output
A messaging sheet used across all deck drafts.

---

## Step 6 - Build the slide-by-slide raw draft

The team should first write the deck in plain text before visual design.

For each slide, define:
- title
- key point
- 3 bullet points max
- chart or visual needed
- source/evidence required

### Output
A text-only deck draft.

---

## Step 7 - Prepare visuals and charts

The team should identify what needs visuals instead of paragraphs.

### Likely visuals
- ecosystem map of tools
- workflow before/after
- product screenshots
- roadmap graphic
- benchmark layer diagram
- market sizing chart
- pricing/ROI example

### Output
A visual requirements list for design.

---

## Step 8 - Convert raw draft into investor style

The team should refine the deck to match investor expectations:
- shorter slide copy
- stronger signal density
- clearer market logic
- sharper differentiation
- stronger business framing

This is where Cursor helps heavily.

---

## Step 9 - Pressure test the narrative

Run mock investor questions such as:
- Why is this venture scale?
- Why is this not just a feature inside existing tools?
- Why will customers trust your verification logic?
- Why is invoice automation your first wedge?
- How defensible is the product?
- What data advantage grows over time?

### Output
A Q&A objection sheet and improved deck copy.

---

## Step 10 - Create multiple deck versions

The team should not rely on one deck only.

### Create
- full investor deck
- short intro deck
- one-page summary
- product demo companion
- founder meeting notes

### Output
A pitch package, not just a slide deck.

---

## 6. Full Cursor / Model Execution Plan

Cursor should be used as a structured copilot, not as a replacement for company judgement.

## Step 1 - Feed Cursor a complete context pack

Before asking for slide writing, give Cursor:
- product summary
- target customer
- biggest pain point
- product roadmap
- integration list
- pricing assumptions
- customer proof
- team background
- fundraising target if relevant

### Best practice
Use one clean master brief document as source of truth.

---

## Step 2 - Ask Cursor to produce category options

Good pitch decks depend on positioning.

Ask Cursor to produce 3 to 5 category framings, for example:
- AI work verification platform
- delivery intelligence and billing infrastructure
- financial operations layer for service teams
- verified time-to-invoice platform

### Output
Choose one primary framing and one backup framing.

---

## Step 3 - Ask Cursor for multiple one-line positioning statements

Examples of prompts:
- Write 10 investor-grade positioning lines
- Write 10 less technical versions
- Write 10 category-creating versions

### Output
Select top 3 options and refine them manually.

---

## Step 4 - Generate the deck skeleton

Ask Cursor to create:
- 10-slide version
- 12-slide version
- 14-slide version

### Output
Pick the version that fits your fundraising stage.

---

## Step 5 - Draft each slide in layers

For every slide, ask Cursor for:
1. headline options
2. short investor bullets
3. evidence suggestions
4. chart or visual suggestions
5. speaker note suggestions

### Rule
Do not accept first-draft wording blindly. Review and tighten every claim.

---

## Step 6 - Ask Cursor to strengthen investor logic

Use prompts like:
- challenge this slide as an investor
- make this slide more venture-backable
- what is missing from this market story
- rewrite this to sound more like a category leader pitch

### Output
Sharper business framing.

---

## Step 7 - Use Cursor for consistency passes

Ask Cursor to review:
- repeated claims
- inconsistent terms
- weak slide transitions
- unsupported claims
- areas that sound like features, not business outcomes

### Output
A tighter, more coherent deck.

---

## Step 8 - Generate investor Q&A prep

Ask Cursor to produce:
- likely objections
- toughest questions
- concise founder answers
- weak points in the current narrative

### Output
An investor prep sheet.

---

## Step 9 - Generate audience-specific versions

Ask Cursor to adapt the same deck for:
- AI-focused investors
- fintech/ops investors
- generalist early-stage investors

### Output
Audience-tuned wording without changing core truth.

---

## Step 10 - Generate supporting collateral

Ask Cursor to create:
- email intro blurb
- meeting follow-up summary
- one-page memo
- FAQ
- product demo script
- beta partner outreach note

### Output
A complete investor communication pack.

---

## 7. Division of Responsibilities

## Team responsibilities
- decide strategy
- approve claims
- provide numbers and proof
- validate market assumptions
- own the product story
- own fundraising target and use of funds

## Cursor responsibilities
- rewrite for clarity
- provide structure options
- compare narrative angles
- compress slide copy
- produce alternative framings
- generate notes, variants, and Q&A support

## Never delegate fully to Cursor
- final market size assumptions
- final traction claims
- legal/compliance claims
- customer commitments
- financial projections

## 9. Execution Sequence

Follow this order:

1. Write the master product brief
2. Define the investor narrative
3. Gather proof and metrics
4. Write the text-only deck
5. Use Cursor to improve framing and compression
6. Build visuals
7. Run investor challenge and objection testing
8. Create short and long versions
9. Finalize speaker notes
10. Package the full pitch kit

---

### Session 1
- lock the narrative
- define target investor type
- confirm product wedge

### Session 2
- gather proof, metrics, and visuals
- write the text-only deck

### Session 3
- use Cursor to sharpen each slide
- remove weak claims
- tighten the business case

### Session 4
- design the deck
- create short version and notes

### Session 5
- mock investor Q&A
- patch weak slides
- finalize final package

---

## 11. What "Investor-Style" Really Means

A true investor-style deck is:
- concise
- high signal
- proof-led
- market-aware
- category-aware
- clear on why this becomes large
- clear on why this team can win

It is not:
- feature-heavy
- too technical
- overloaded with generic AI language
- vague about ROI
- vague about the first customer wedge
